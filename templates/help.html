🔑 Logging In & Getting Started

When you open IMCS for the first time, you'll land on the login screen. Here's exactly what to do.

1
Enter your Company Name
Type the name of your organisation exactly as it was registered. This is how IMCS keeps your data separate from other companies.
2
Enter your Login ID and Password
Your Login ID was given to you by your administrator (or you chose it when registering). Type your password - it is case-sensitive.
3
Click "Login"
You'll be taken straight to your Dashboard. If you see an error, double-check your Company Name and Login ID - they must match exactly.
4
First time? Click "Register"
If you're setting up IMCS for the first time, click Register on the login page. Enter a Company Name (this creates your company's workspace), choose a Login ID, your name, and a password of at least 6 characters.
💡 Tip: Once you're logged in, IMCS remembers your session. You won't need to log in again unless you click Sign Out or close the browser.
[!]️ No data yet? After your first login, your dashboard will be empty. Click the Demo Data button in the dashboard header, then Generate Demo Data to instantly fill the system with sample machines and readings so you can explore.
🏠 Your Dashboard - The Big Picture

The Dashboard is the first thing you see after logging in. Think of it as your control room - it shows you everything that's happening across all your machines at a glance.

📦
Total Machines
How many machines are registered in your system right now.
⚡
Average Efficiency
The average performance percentage across all your machines. Higher is better - aim for above 80%.
[!]️
Active Alerts
Problems that need your attention. This number should ideally be zero.
💚
System Health
An overall score for how well your operation is running. Excellent means everything is fine.
▶
The charts update automatically every 60 seconds
You don't need to refresh the page. The live green dot next to "Auto-refresh every 60s" in the header confirms this is working. You can also click Refresh any time for an immediate update.
▶
Performance Trend chart
Shows how your machines' efficiency has changed over the last 7 days. A rising line is good. A sudden drop may mean a machine needs attention.
▶
Machine Status chart (doughnut)
A quick visual of how many machines are Running, Idle, Under Maintenance, or Offline. Hover over any slice to see the exact number and percentage.
▶
Alerts Trend chart
Shows how many alerts were raised each day over the last 2 weeks, split by severity. A spike here means something unusual happened on that day.
▶
Recent Alerts panel
Lists your most recent unresolved alerts. Click Ack next to any alert to mark it as reviewed and resolved. You can add a note explaining what you did.
▶
Machine table at the bottom
A full list of all your machines with their current status and efficiency. Use the search box to find a specific machine quickly. Click View to see full details for any machine.
▶
Create a Maintenance Task from here
Click the 🔧 Maintenance button in the top header. A form will appear - pick the machine, describe what needs doing, set a priority, and optionally assign it to a technician.
🏭 Managing Your Machines

The Machinery page is your complete list of every machine in your facility. You can add new machines, check their current status, and drill into detailed information for each one.

1
Go to Machinery
Click Operations -> Machinery in the left sidebar. You'll see a summary row at the top showing how many machines are Running, Idle, Under Maintenance, and Offline.
2
Find a machine quickly
Type anything into the search box - the machine's name, type, location, or status. The list filters instantly as you type.
3
Add a new machine
Click + Add Machine in the top-right. Fill in:
* Machine Name - e.g. "Lathe-01" or "Compressor A"
* Type - e.g. "CNC", "Lathe", "Conveyor"
* Location - e.g. "Shop Floor A", "Warehouse 2"
* Status - what state it's currently in
Then click Add Machine to save it.
💡 All three fields (Name, Type, Location) are required. You can always update the status later from the machine details page.
4
View a machine's full details
Click anywhere on a machine's row (or the Details button). You'll see its sensor readings, efficiency history over time, and all past maintenance work done on it.
🔧 Scheduling & Tracking Maintenance

Maintenance tasks help you keep track of servicing, repairs, and inspections. Every task moves through three stages: Open -> In Progress -> Completed.

1
Open the Maintenance page
Click Operations -> Maintenance in the sidebar. The orange number badge on this link tells you how many tasks are currently open and waiting.
2
Create a new task
Click + New Task. A form appears:
* Machine - pick from the dropdown (all your registered machines appear here)
* Description - describe what needs to be done, e.g. "Replace drive belt"
* Priority - High (urgent), Medium (this week), or Low (routine)
* Scheduled Date - when you plan to do it
* Technician - who will do the work (optional)
Click Create Task when done.
3
Update a task as work progresses
Find the task in the list and click Update. Change the status from Open to In Progress when work begins, then to Completed when it's done. You can also reassign the technician here.
4
Filter the task list
Use the filter bar to show only tasks with a certain status (e.g. just "Open" tasks) or priority (e.g. just "High" priority). You can also search by machine name or description.

✅ Good practice

  • Set a scheduled date so nothing gets forgotten
  • Mark tasks "In Progress" when work starts
  • Always mark tasks "Completed" when done
  • Assign a technician so responsibility is clear

❌ Avoid

  • Leaving tasks as "Open" indefinitely
  • Creating duplicate tasks for the same job
  • Using "High" priority for everything
[!]️ Dealing with Alerts

Alerts are the system's way of telling you something needs your attention. The red number badge on the Alerts link in the sidebar shows how many unresolved alerts you have right now.

1
Open the Alerts page
Click Monitoring -> Alerts in the sidebar. By default you'll see only unresolved alerts - the ones that still need action.
2
Understand the severity
🔴 Critical - Something is seriously wrong. Act immediately.
🟠 Warning - A problem is developing. Check it soon.
🔵 Info - Just a notification. No urgent action needed.
3
Acknowledge (resolve) an alert
Click the Acknowledge button next to an alert. A small window appears showing the alert details. You can type a note about what you did (e.g. "Checked sensor, recalibrated"). Click Acknowledge & Resolve to close it. The alert disappears from your list and the badge count goes down.
4
See all alerts (including resolved ones)
Use the filter bar at the top of the Alerts page. Change the Status filter from "Unacknowledged" to "All" to see every alert ever raised, including ones already resolved.
💡 Good habit: Check your alerts every morning. Resolving Critical alerts the same day they appear keeps your operation running smoothly.
📊 Charts & Reports

IMCS gives you several ways to visualise and export your data.

▶
Save any chart as an image
Every chart on the Dashboard has a small 📥 button in its title bar. Click it to download the chart as a PNG image you can paste into reports or emails.
▶
Upload your own data (CSV)
Scroll to the bottom of the Dashboard. You'll see a CSV Data Visualization area. Drag any spreadsheet file saved as CSV onto it (or click to browse). IMCS will automatically draw a chart from your data - great for visualising production logs or external sensor exports.
💡 Your CSV must have column headers in the first row. The first column becomes the X-axis. Any columns with numbers are plotted as lines.
▶
Reports page
Click Reporting -> Reports in the sidebar for downloadable summaries and analytics about your machines and operations over time.
🗂️ Getting Around the Application

The sidebar on the left is how you move between different parts of IMCS. Here's how to get the most out of it.

▶
Collapse the sidebar for more space
Click the ◀ button at the top of the sidebar to shrink it to just icons. Click ▶ to bring it back. IMCS remembers your preference - it stays collapsed even after you navigate to a different page.
▶
Collapse individual menu groups
Click on any section heading (like "Operations" or "Monitoring") to hide or show the links underneath. Useful if you only use certain parts of the system.
▶
Search the navigation
Type in the search box at the top of the sidebar to instantly filter the navigation links. For example, type "alert" to jump straight to the Alerts link.
▶
Live notification badges
The red badge on Alerts shows unresolved alerts. The orange badge on Maintenance shows open tasks. These update every time you load a page so you always know what needs attention.
▶
Sign out safely
Click Sign Out at the very bottom of the sidebar when you're done. This clears your session so nobody else can access your data on a shared computer.
🚦 What Do the Colours Mean?

Machine Status Colours

Green - Running · Machine is working normally
Teal - Idle · Machine is on but not producing
Amber - Maintenance · Machine is being serviced
Red - Offline · Machine is off or unreachable

Alert Severity Colours

Red - Critical · Act immediately
Orange - Warning · Check soon
Blue - Info · No urgent action needed

Maintenance Priority Colours

Red - High · Do it today
Orange - Medium · Do it this week
Green - Low · Schedule at your convenience
❓ Common Questions
I just logged in and the dashboard is empty. What do I do? ▼
That's normal for a brand-new account! Click the Datasets button in the dashboard header, then click Generate Demo Data. Enter how many machines you want (try 5) and how many days of history (try 30), then click OK. Your dashboard will fill up with sample data within seconds so you can explore all the features.
The machine dropdown in the Maintenance form is empty. ▼
You need to add at least one machine first. Go to Machinery -> Add Machine and register your first machine. Once saved, it will appear in the dropdown when you create a maintenance task. Alternatively, generate demo data (see above) which creates machines automatically.
How do I know if a machine needs attention? ▼
Watch for two things: (1) A red alert badge on the Alerts link in the sidebar - click it to see what's wrong. (2) A machine showing Offline or Maintenance status in red or amber on the Dashboard or Machinery page. Low efficiency (below 60%) on the charts is also a warning sign.
I acknowledged an alert by mistake. Can I undo it? ▼
Once an alert is acknowledged it is marked as resolved in the system. To see it again, go to the Alerts page and change the Status filter to "All" - you'll see all alerts including resolved ones. You cannot un-acknowledge an alert, but you can create a new alert manually via the API if needed.
Can my colleagues use the same account? ▼
Each person should have their own Login ID. When registering, use the same Company Name as your organisation - this puts everyone in the same workspace so you all see the same machines, alerts, and tasks. Ask your administrator to register accounts for each team member.
The charts look empty or show "0" everywhere. ▼
Charts need data to display. If you've just registered, generate demo data first. If you have real machines but no sensor readings, the charts will show zeros until sensor data is recorded. Try clicking Refresh on the dashboard. If the problem persists, check your internet connection - Chart.js loads from a CDN.
How do I remove demo data when I'm ready to use real data? ▼
On the Dashboard, click Datasets in the header, then click Clear Demo Data. Confirm the prompt. All demo machines, sensors, alerts, and readings will be deleted. This cannot be undone, so only do this when you're ready to start entering your real machines.
I forgot my password. How do I reset it? ▼
Currently, password reset must be done by your system administrator who has access to the server. Contact your IT team or the person who set up IMCS for your organisation. They can update your password directly in the system.
The sidebar collapse button doesn't seem to work. ▼
Make sure you're clicking the small ◀ arrow button at the top-right corner of the sidebar (not the section headers). If it still doesn't respond, try refreshing the page. The sidebar state is saved in your browser - clearing your browser's local storage will reset it to expanded.
